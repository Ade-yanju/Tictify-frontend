How to Sell Event Tickets Online in Nigeria: A Beginner's Guide

By the Tictify Team · Published June 2026

For years, selling event tickets in Nigeria meant printing paper stubs, collecting bank transfers, replying to "I have sent it" messages all day, and hoping nobody copied your ticket design. It worked, but it was stressful, easy to defraud, and hard to scale. Selling online fixes all of that. If you have only ever done manual tickets, this guide walks you through moving to online sales — how payments work, how QR tickets protect you, how you get paid, and exactly how to launch your first event.

Why move away from cash and manual tickets

Manual selling has three quiet problems. First, it eats your time — every buyer becomes a conversation. Second, it is easy to cheat: paper tickets get photographed and reused, and "transfer" screenshots can be faked. Third, you have no real record, so you never truly know how many tickets you have sold until the gate is chaos. Online ticketing removes all three. Guests pay themselves, each ticket is unique, and you get a live count of every sale. You spend your energy on the event, not on chasing payments.

How online payments work in Nigeria

The heart of online ticketing is letting guests pay instantly and confirm themselves. On Tictify, payments run through Paystack, which means your guests can pay with:

  • Debit card — the fastest option, confirmed in seconds.
  • Bank transfer — for guests who prefer moving money from their banking app, with automatic confirmation once it lands.

The moment payment succeeds, the system issues the ticket automatically. There is no waiting for you to check your account, no screenshots to verify, and no arguments about whether money was received. This alone removes the single biggest headache of running events in Nigeria.

QR-code tickets: your protection against fakes

Every ticket sold online comes as a unique QR code delivered to the guest by email. At the gate, you scan it and the system instantly tells you whether it is valid and whether it has already been used. Because each code can only be admitted once, a screenshotted or forwarded ticket is caught the second time it is scanned. This is what finally kills the "one ticket, five entries" problem that costs organisers so much money. We explain the mechanics in detail in our article on how QR-code tickets stop gate fraud, including how scanning works even with a weak network connection.

Getting paid to your Nigerian bank account

Selling online only matters if the money reaches you cleanly. When you set a ticket price on Tictify, that price is what you keep — platform and payment fees are added on top and paid by the guest at checkout. Your earnings sit in your dashboard as tickets sell, and you withdraw them to your ordinary Nigerian bank account. There is no foreign account, no crypto, and no waiting on a third party to "release" your money by hand. You can watch sales climb in real time and cash out to the bank you already use.

The price you set is the price you keep. Fees ride on top for the guest, so your planning maths stays simple. For help choosing your numbers, see our guide on how to price event tickets in Nigeria.

Step by step: launch your first event online

Here is the whole journey from idea to selling, without any technical background required:

  1. Create your account. Sign up on Tictify with your email — it takes a couple of minutes.
  2. Set up the event. Add the title, date, venue, a short description and a good cover image. A clear, attractive event page sells more tickets than a plain one.
  3. Add your ticket types. Create your tiers — for example Regular, VIP and Table — and set the price and quantity for each. Cap the limited ones so scarcity is real.
  4. Add early-bird or discount codes (optional). Release a cheaper early batch or create promo codes for promoters and ambassadors.
  5. Connect your payout bank details. Tell the system which Nigerian bank account should receive your earnings.
  6. Publish and share. Your event gets a link you can drop on WhatsApp Status, Instagram, TikTok and flyers. Every share can lead straight to a sale.
  7. Sell and track. Watch your dashboard as tickets sell, and adjust your promotion based on what is moving.
  8. Scan at the gate. On event day, use any phone to scan guests in quickly and securely.

Promote what you have built

An online ticket page is only as good as the number of people who see it. Once your event is live, the link becomes the centre of all your marketing. Put it in your bio, in your WhatsApp broadcasts, and on every flyer. If you want a full playbook, read our guide on how to promote your event in Nigeria — the two work hand in hand, because effortless buying plus consistent promotion is what fills a venue.

Common worries, answered

  • "My guests are not tech-savvy." If they can send a WhatsApp message, they can tap a link and pay. Card and transfer are things most Nigerians already do daily.
  • "What if someone shares their ticket?" The QR code only admits one entry. A shared ticket is caught on the second scan.
  • "How do I know it is really selling?" Your dashboard shows every sale live, so you always know exactly where you stand.
  • "What about network at the gate?" Scanning is designed to keep working even when the venue's connection is poor.
